Proposal · British Columbia

Medicine to the community, not another trip to town.

MILKRUN is developing an on-demand drone logistics service for remote communities in British Columbia.

The first use case is simple: when a patient is prescribed medication during a community clinic, deliver the prescription from the Pemberton area to the community instead of requiring the patient or their family to make another trip into town.

The problem

Primary care can reach the community. The medication still has to come from town.

Doctors and nurses are increasingly providing care directly in remote communities around Pemberton and Mount Currie.

But when a prescription is issued during a community visit, the medication is still prepared by a pharmacy in Pemberton. The patient is contacted when it is ready. From there, the patient or their family has to arrange the trip into town to collect it.

For ordinary prescriptions this is inconvenient. For something like an antibiotic that should be started within hours, it can become a genuine access problem.

MILKRUN is being developed to provide that missing logistics leg.

The pilot

Start with one useful route.

The first MILKRUN pilot would test same-day delivery of ordinary prescription medication from the Pemberton area to a designated receiving point in one remote community.

How it works
01

Dispensed

The pharmacy dispenses, labels and seals the prescription as usual.

02

Transported

The sealed package is handed to the approved RPAS operator and flown to the designated community receiving site.

03

Received

A trained local person receives the package and places it into the agreed secure handoff process.

04

Collected

The patient is notified and collects the prescription locally.

MILKRUN would provide the transport layer. Prescribing, dispensing and clinical decisions remain with the appropriate healthcare and pharmacy professionals.

What we would carry
Initial use case

Prescription medication

Small, time-sensitive prescriptions where avoiding an additional trip into Pemberton creates a clear benefit. Antibiotics are the reference case for the first pilot.

Later

Lab specimens

A return flight could eventually move appropriately packaged specimens from a community back toward the laboratory system. That could turn the service into a two-way healthcare logistics network.

Not in the first pilot

Complex payloads

Controlled substances, blood products, refrigerated biologics and other complex payloads are not part of the initial proposal.

Community

Built with the communities it serves.

MILKRUN should create local capability as well as move packages. The proposed operating model includes paid local roles in each participating community.

If the selected aircraft requires battery exchange or charging at the destination, that could also be handled locally after appropriate training.

As the network develops, the aim would be to create pathways into RPAS training and operational roles rather than permanently importing all aviation staff from outside the communities.

Proposed local role

Community Logistics Operator

A trained local person who could support:

  • 01Landing-site checks before arrival
  • 02Receiving and removing the sealed package
  • 03Recording receipt
  • 04Secure storage
  • 05Coordinating patient collection
  • 06Reporting local weather or site conditions
  • 07Basic ground equipment support

Make the first aircraft local too.

If a pilot goes ahead, MILKRUN would like local youth to help name the first aircraft and design its livery or landing-site identity. This follows an approach already used in Indigenous healthcare drone projects in British Columbia.

Aircraft

The aircraft comes second.

MILKRUN is evaluating existing commercial long-range VTOL platforms rather than designing a proprietary aircraft.

A VTOL fixed-wing platform is currently the leading architecture because the proposed routes require efficient long-distance flight without relying on a runway.

Selection criteria — the aircraft will be chosen against the actual mission

Range

Enough usable range with reserve for the selected route.

Payload

Small prescription packages rather than heavy freight.

Weather

A realistic operating envelope for coastal BC conditions.

Communications

Reliable command-and-control and appropriate contingency behaviour.

Regulation

Suitable for the Canadian operating pathway required by the route.

Cost

Low enough that the service makes sense relative to the problem being solved.

Why this is worth testing

This is not a new category of technology.

Medical drone delivery has already been demonstrated in British Columbia, including work with Indigenous communities. The remaining question for MILKRUN is more practical:

Can this particular healthcare problem be served reliably, safely and economically on these particular routes?

That is what the pilot is intended to establish.
